# Guandan Studio — V0.1 Product Plan

Status: Product design draft; no implementation at this stage.  
Date: 2026-10-22  
Chinese version: [简体中文](V0.1-PLAN.zh-CN.md)

## 1. Positioning and Goals

A bilingual Chinese/English Guandan hand arrangement and practice website hosted on GitHub Pages.

Core flow: **Deal → Arrange Cards → Review feedback and alternatives → Advance play → Rearrange → Review and retry**.

Help players compare arrangements of the same hand and understand tradeoffs between the number of groups to shed, control, weak singles, and wild-card flexibility. Use **Arrange Cards** consistently for the arrangement action.

V0.1 excludes accounts, multiplayer, public leaderboards, cross-device synchronization, and a full leveling match. Tribute, returning tribute, and progression through the Ace level are deferred.

## 2. Rules and Heart-Level Wild Cards

Use two decks, totaling 108 cards, with 27 cards per player. Opposite seats are partners. An independent practice deal defaults to a random level rank from 2 through Ace, with manual selection available. This is separate from a continuous leveling match.

**The hearts of the current level rank are wild cards, and must be supported throughout rules, arrangement, hints, evaluation, and replay.**

For example, at level 7:

- Both ♥7 cards are wild. They may substitute for non-joker cards when combined with other cards into a legal pattern.
- A ♥7 played alone counts as a level card; it cannot be declared as an arbitrary single card.
- ♠7, ♣7, and ♦7 are ordinary level cards without wild-card substitution.
- Support combinations using one or both wild cards, subject to the selected ruleset.
- Show the physical card and its assigned identity, for example `♥7 → ♠9`.
- A wild card may remain unassigned. Applying a group sets its current assignment; dismantling that group permits reassignment. A declaration cannot change after the cards are played.
- History preserves physical identities, substituted identities, and the declared pattern.

Supported patterns: singles, pairs, triples, full houses, five-card straights, three consecutive pairs, two consecutive triples, matching-rank bombs, straight flushes, and the four-joker bomb.

Before implementation, publish a rule table covering Ace, 2, and level ranks in consecutive patterns; wild-card restrictions; bomb ordering; transfer of the lead after a player goes out; and deal termination. References may differ, so a strategy article must not define the rules engine. V0.1 supports one explicitly named and versioned ruleset.

## 5. Arrange Cards Interaction

### 5.1 Default Display

- Follow the supplied screenshot: stack equal ranks vertically and place different ranks horizontally.
- Default to higher ranks on the left; allow reversing direction. Use a consistent suit order within each rank.
- Clearly mark jokers, level cards, and wild cards.
- Rank View shows quantities; Group View shows planned combinations.
- Preserve groups when switching views. Display sorting alone must not change arrangement scores.

### 5.2 Grouping Controls

Allow selecting or dragging cards to create a group, dismantling groups, locking groups, undo, redo, restoring rank order, arranging unlocked cards automatically, and submitting an arrangement.

Keep ungrouped cards in a separate area. Each physical card can belong to only one group; identical cards from the two decks remain distinct entities. Explain invalid groups specifically. When multiple legal interpretations exist, let the player confirm the pattern and wild-card assignments.

Locks constrain automatic arrangement, not legal play. Automated play may dismantle a group when appropriate and must record why. Previewing a suggestion does not move cards; applying it changes the grouping.

## 6. Straight Flush Hints

Provide four suit buttons: ♠, ♥, ♣, and ♦.

| State | Display |
|---|---|
| Natural straight flush available | Solid suit with candidate count |
| Wild cards required | Solid suit with wild-card badge |
| No legal candidate | Translucent suit plus an explicit unavailable indicator |
| Multiple candidates | Repeated clicks cycle candidates and show position |

Find natural candidates and candidates using one or two wild cards. Highlight five specific physical cards and show the sequence, assignments, and wild-card cost. If a suit has both natural and assisted candidates, show natural ones first and label the current candidate type.

Separate **Preview** from **Apply**. Explain when candidates share cards and cannot coexist. Identify locked groups that would need to be dismantled. Deduplicate candidates by distinct physical card sets and declarations to avoid inflated counts.

Hint modes: always available, available after submission, or off.

## 7. Evaluation and Explanations

Use explainable heuristics. Do not present scores as win probabilities.

| Evaluation | Presentation | Factors |
|---|---|---|
| Hand potential | Strong / medium / weak with factors | Control resources, combination potential, weak singles, wild cards |
| Arrangement quality | Indicative 0–100 score with factors | Groups to shed, weak singles, retained control, structural integrity, wild-card flexibility |
| Current position | Favorable / balanced / unfavorable with reasons | Lead, remaining structure, public threats, partnership opportunities |

Arrangement quality primarily compares alternatives for the same hand, rather than ranking different deals. Calibrate weights against example positions before fixing the formula. Fewer groups, more bombs, or a straight flush must never be the sole objective.

Prioritize two strengths, two costs, and one or two alternatives with their applicable conditions. For example: an arrangement removes one group but uses both wild cards; another retains a wild card at the cost of an extra group.

Automatic arrangement should explore shedding efficiency, control, and flexibility. Unless exhaustive search is completed, label results as suggestions rather than an optimal solution or minimum group count. Apply a computation budget and return the best suggestions found so far when needed.

Update the position after each action and the structure evaluation when the player's hand changes. A drop in remaining hand potential after correctly playing strong cards is not automatically an error.

## 8. Strategy Teaching Library

Treat the user's 100 supplied maxims as contextual teaching material and retain attribution. V0.1 selects approximately 10–15 arrangement topics: fewer groups, weak singles, the cost of straights, wild-card flexibility, straight-flush versus bomb tradeoffs, and dynamic regrouping.

Each strategy card includes the original maxim, a plain-language explanation, applicable conditions, exceptions, and an example. Maxims trigger teaching feedback, not automatic judgments of correctness.

Do not turn unsupported probability claims into system conclusions. Timing-based reads must not drive computer decisions or animation hints. Remaining card counts must be considered alongside patterns, the lead, public history, and partner position.

The full 100-topic library and a dedicated strategy experiment mode are deferred.

## 9. Automated Play

Three basic computer players control the other seats. The user may play manually or automate all seats.

| Control | Behavior |
|---|---|
| Next Action | Execute one seat's play or pass |
| Run to My Turn | Advance until the player must act |
| Auto Play | Run continuously with pause and speed controls |

Computer players support legal responses, basic control preservation, shedding loose cards, and simple partnership play. Label their level as Basic Strategy. Automated play considers current groups but may dismantle them as the position changes.

After each action, update public history, card counts, and position feedback. Explain key changes briefly without modal interruptions. Allow pausing and rearranging before the player's action.

## 10. Information Boundaries

- Visibility controls do not change computer permissions. Computers use only their own cards and public information.
- Live advice and evaluation in hidden-hand mode must not read hidden cards.
- Partner and opponent explanations have separate visibility controls and must not reveal hidden information.
- All-hands-visible mode may provide global analysis with its basis labeled. Revealing cards transitions to review.
- Label analysis as Hand Analysis, Public Information Analysis, All-Hands Analysis, or Revealed-Hand Review.
- Artificial animation delays must be independent of hand contents.

## 11. History, Review, and Persistence

Record the seat, play or pass, wild-card declarations, and remaining count for every action. Support replay from the beginning, viewing past states, retrying from an action, restarting a deal, and comparing initial arrangements with later changes.

Retries create branches and preserve the original attempt. Label post-reveal branches as review rather than hidden-hand practice. Save preferences, deals, and progress in the current browser, with a clear-data control.

Reproducibility requires the deal generator version, seed, ruleset version, level rank, opening seat, and relevant initial settings. Reproducing automated actions additionally requires the computer strategy version and its random conditions.

## 13. GitHub Pages and Runtime

Build a static website running locally in the browser. Dealing, rules, strategies, and evaluation execute on the client. Run heavier arrangement analysis in background tasks with computation budgets to keep interaction responsive.

GitHub Pages hosts the site and static assets. No backend or paid model API is required. Hidden hands support self-directed practice; the complete deal exists on the client, so V0.1 does not provide competitive anti-cheating guarantees. Synchronization and multiplayer would require future services.

## 14. Delivery Sequence

1. Finalize the rule table, bilingual terms, and scoring examples.
2. Build dealing, stacked display, and manual grouping.
3. Add wild-card handling, straight-flush candidates, and suggested arrangements with evaluation.
4. Add basic automated play, dynamic updates, and information boundaries.
5. Add history, local persistence, bilingual coverage, and mobile checks.
6. Verify and deploy to GitHub Pages.

## 15. Acceptance Criteria

| Area | Completion criterion |
|---|---|
| Dealing | Exactly 27 cards per seat; no missing or excess cards; reproducible deals |
| Rules | Pattern recognition, comparison, wild cards, and lead transfer follow the published ruleset |
| Display and grouping | Correct stacking; create, dismantle, lock, and undo without card loss or reuse |
| Wild cards | Correct one/two-card substitution; no joker substitution; singles remain level cards; traceable declarations |
| Straight flushes | Correct natural/assisted candidates; clear duplicate-card, overlap, and lock handling |
| Evaluation | Concrete tradeoffs; sorting does not change scores; scores are not win probabilities |
| Play | Complete a legal deal with passes, players going out, lead transfer, and termination |
| Information | Live advice and other computers do not use hidden cards or leak them through explanations |
| Review | Replay, branching retries, and restart work; reveal status is clear |
| Language and devices | Full bilingual flow; switching preserves state; core desktop/mobile interactions work |
| Deployment | Directly accessible on GitHub Pages without accounts or a backend |

Use targeted fixed deals covering two wild cards, competing straight-flush/bomb arrangements, identical physical card copies, consecutive patterns involving level cards, and hidden-information boundaries.
