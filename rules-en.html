THE ESSENTIALS · ENGLISH GUIDE

How to play Guandan.

A partnership game of combinations, timing, and helping your teammate finish first. Learn the rules of Guandan (掼蛋, guàn dàn), then try them at the practice table.

4 players2 teams108 cards27 cards each

This guide follows the Studio 0.1 practice rules. Local and tournament variants may differ. Chinese terms are followed by Mandarin pinyin with tone marks.

01 / THE GOAL

Play as a pair. Finish as a team.

Four players use two standard decks, including both pairs of jokers. Each player receives 27 cards. The player opposite you is your partner (对家, duì jiā); the players on either side are your opponents.

The aim is to shed all your cards in legal combinations. The first player to finish is the first finisher (头游, tóu yóu). Their team wins the deal, and the partner’s finishing position matters in a full match. Helping your partner can be more valuable than winning every exchange yourself.

02 / CARD RANKS

One rank gets promoted each deal.

Every deal has a level rank (级牌, jí pái). Outside consecutive patterns, cards of that rank sit above aces and below jokers.

Highest → lowest

Red joker → black joker → level rank → A → K → Q → J → 10 … 2, skipping the level rank in its usual position.

For example, when playing at level 7, a single 7 beats an ace, but loses to either joker. Suits do not break ties. Two identical cards from different decks are still two separate physical cards.

In a straight, consecutive pairs, or consecutive triples, the level rank returns to its natural position. At level 7, 5–6–7–8–9 is still a valid straight.

03 / THE IMPORTANT EXCEPTION

♥ Heart-level cards are wild.

The two hearts of the level rank are wild cards (配牌, pèi pái), also called 逢人配 (féng rén pèi). At level 7, both ♥7 cards are wild. The other six 7s are ordinary level cards.

  • A wild card can replace a non-joker card when forming a legal combination.
  • One or both wild cards may be used in the same combination.
  • A wild card cannot stand for a red or black joker.
  • Played alone, it is simply a level card. In this practice ruleset, the two wild cards played alone together form a pair of the level rank.
  • Once played, the declared combination and wild-card assignments are fixed.
EXAMPLE · LEVEL 7
♠5   ♠6   ♥7 → ♠7   ♠8   ♠9

The ♥7 completes a spade straight flush. You could instead use that same wild card elsewhere—but never in two combinations at once.

Wild cards create choices, not an automatic “best” arrangement. Completing a straight flush may mean giving up a useful bomb or leaving awkward singles.

04 / LEGAL COMBINATIONS

Recognize the shape before comparing the rank.

An ordinary response must have the same pattern and number of cards as the current play, with a higher rank. A pair does not beat a single, and a full house does not beat a straight. Bombs are the exception.

Aces in sequences

Ace may be low or high, but cannot wrap around: A–2–3–4–5 and 10–J–Q–K–A are valid; Q–K–A–2–3 is not. Studio also allows AA–22–33 and AAA–222 at the low end of consecutive pairs and triples. Jokers cannot appear in sequences.

Bomb order, from weakest to strongest

Four-card bomb < five-card bomb < straight flush < six-card bomb < seven-card bomb < eight-card bomb < nine-card bomb < ten-card bomb < four-joker bomb.

A bomb beats any ordinary pattern. Between equal-size matching-rank bombs, compare the rank, with the level rank promoted. Between straight flushes, compare the natural sequence: A–2–3–4–5 is lowest and 10–J–Q–K–A is highest. Equal combinations do not beat each other.

05 / TAKING TURNS

Lead, respond, or pass.

Play moves counterclockwise. At this table: you → right opponent → partner → left opponent.

  1. The player with the lead plays any legal combination. They cannot pass on a fresh lead.
  2. Each following player either beats the current play or passes (不出, bù chū). Passing is allowed even when a stronger response is available.
  3. If somebody beats the play, the others get another opportunity to respond—including players who passed earlier.
  4. Once every other player still holding cards passes, the last player to play successfully starts a new exchange with any legal pattern.
A short example

You lead 6–6. The next player may play a higher pair, a bomb, or pass. They cannot respond with a single ace or a triple. If everyone passes, you regain the lead and may switch to a different pattern.

06 / FINISHING & PARTNERSHIP

Your last cards can give your partner the lead.

When a player runs out of cards, their final play can still be beaten. If nobody beats it, their partner inherits the next lead. This is inheriting the lead (接风, jiē fēng).

Studio ends the deal when both teammates finish first and second, or when three players have finished. The team of the first finisher wins. If both opponents finish last, that is a double loss (双下, shuāng xià).

In a full match, teams advance through level ranks, and later deals may involve tribute (进贡, jìn gòng) and returning tribute (还贡, huán gòng). Studio V0.1 focuses on independent practice deals and does not implement those match procedures.

07 / YOUR FIRST PRACTICE DEAL

Arrange, compare, then play.

Arranging cards (理牌, lǐ pái) means deciding how your cards might work together. Fewer planned plays can help, but retaining control and keeping a wild card flexible may be worth an extra group.

  1. Check the level rank and identify any heart-level wild cards.
  2. Select cards and choose Group cards, or inspect an Auto arrange suggestion.
  3. Use the suit buttons to preview straight flushes. Repeated clicks cycle candidates; Apply group uses the selected cards.
  4. Choose Evaluate to inspect your arrangement. The score is a heuristic reference, not a win probability.
  5. Play selected cards, advance to your next turn, and rearrange when the position changes. Open History to revisit an earlier decision.

You can reveal your partner or opponents for study. Computer players still use only their own hands and public information. Revealing cards marks the deal as a review.
